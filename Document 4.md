## 1. Case Statement: Why `IGWelfare` Needs Physical, External, and Civic-Sector Closure

`IGWelfare` currently solves a 60-year multiphase transition path (`SS0 -> SS1 -> SS2`) across four domestic regions (`Rural`, `Small`, `Large`, `Auckland`) plus an endogenous emigration outside option (`Abroad`) in 1–2 minutes ([Document 2A](Document%202A.md), [Document 2B](Document%202B.md)). That computational headroom creates an opportunity to confront the four structural blind spots that still separate domestic social-investment models from full macroeconomic closure:

1. **The Small Open Economy (SOE) & Tradable Composition Constraint:** As established in our coalition synthesis ([Document 3](Document%203.md)), domestic human-capital, housing, and enterprise reforms only overcome the Long-Term Fiscal Model (LTFM) sinkhole [Ref 31, 32] when coupled across the human lifecycle rather than pursued in single-lane silos. Yet a central-bank or Treasury reviewer can still ask how a land- and emissions-constrained commodity exporter funds an 18.8-year Phase-1 (`SS1`) incubation window—or sustains a **+150% expansion in national defense spending** (from ~1.2% to 3.0% of GDP, +NZD 8.0B/yr [Ref 59])—without triggering a current account blowout, real exchange rate crowding-out, or widening sovereign risk spreads [Ref 4, 10, 40].
2. **The Biophysical Energy Illusion:** New Zealand routinely conflates an ~85.5%–88.5% renewable *electricity* grid with clean energy independence, ignoring that roughly 60%–68% of total consumer and primary energy consists of fossil fuels—predominantly imported liquid petroleum products (~NZD 19.5B/yr) alongside depleting Taranaki gas and thermal coal firming [Ref 39, 46]. Simultaneously, 2024–2026 industrial curtailments (pulp, paper, and timber mill closures during wholesale power spikes) demonstrate that energy is not a minor, smoothly substitutable 4% cost share [Ref 9]; it is a physical capacity constraint governed by domestic hydro-firming bottlenecks and vertically integrated gentailer market power [Ref 39, 49].
3. **The Monolithic Non-Tradable & Public-Provision Fallacy:** Treating early childhood wraparound (`G_ece_stage1`), adolescent mentoring (`G_ece_stage2`), hospitals, water networks, roading, grassroots NGOs, and sovereign defense purely as fiscal budget lines—or lumping them inside generic private non-tradables—hides real general-equilibrium resource competition. Scaling human-capital services requires hiring real teachers, nurses, and *Whānau Ora* navigators in regional labor markets (~26%–27% of New Zealand's paid workforce sits in public and for-purpose delivery [Ref 30, 42]), while scaling sovereign defense drains foreign exchange on imported platforms and competes directly for technical engineering talent [Ref 59].
4. **The National-Accounts Care & Volunteer Blind Spot:** Leaving non-market household caregiving and community volunteering bundled inside an undifferentiated "leisure" residual creates a classic Marilyn Waring accounting distortion [Ref 30, 47]. Shifting early childhood or elder care between *kuia*/community networks and state/market provision alters measured System of National Accounts (SNA) GDP even when real care inputs are unchanged, while obscuring how incompressible regional energy and housing bills squeeze unpaid caregiving time [Ref 11, 23, 28, 46, 47].

Rather than treating small-open-economy sectoral trade (Australia's *OLGA* [Ref 57]), biophysical exergy transition (*Keen, Ayres, & Standish* [Ref 9] and *Labrousse & Perdereau* [Ref 23]), polycentric public/NGO and defense production (*Ostrom* [Ref 29], *Baumol* & *Atkinson* [Ref 58]), and unpaid care (*Waring* [Ref 47] and *Gronau* [Ref 28]) as competing model expansions, they form **two orthogonal, non-compounding upgrades**: an outer 5-sector general-equilibrium production and external-balance block, paired with an intratemporal household time-and-subsistence block. Consistent with the dimension-free conditioning principles in [Document 2T](Document%202T.md), both upgrades leave the household endogenous state tensor untouched (`1.0x` state-space memory multiplier) and preserve sub-5-minute 60-year transition solves.

## 2. Economic Theory, Sectoral Boundaries, and Empirical Justification

### 2.1 Biophysical Exergy Activation vs. the Neoclassical KLE Fallacy (Keen & Ayres)

Standard CGE and OLG climate models append energy via a nested Capital-Labor-Energy (`KLE`) function governed by Solow's cost-share theorem. Because energy expenditure represents ~3%–5% of GDP, conventional models assign energy an output elasticity near $`\gamma \approx 0.04`$, implying that a 10% physical energy shortfall reduces aggregate output by a trivial ~0.4% to 0.7% [Ref 9].

Following Robert Ayres and Steve Keen (Keen, Ayres, & Standish, 2019; Ayres & Warr, 2009 [Ref 9]), physical capital and labor cannot perform work without energy throughput converted into useful physical work (**exergy**). Energy activates capital rather than substituting for it. Once regional plant, machinery, and public facilities are installed, short-run substitutability between capital-labor bundles and useful exergy approaches zero ($`\sigma_{KL,X} \to 0`$, consistent with Hassler, Krusell, & Olovsson, 2021 [Ref 10]). Distinguishing raw primary energy from useful exergy exposes the physical macroeconomic engine of electrification:

* **Imported Fossil Fuels ($`F`$):** Internal combustion vehicles and industrial/institutional fossil boilers suffer severe Second-Law conversion losses ($`e_F \in \lbrack 0.25, 0.35 \rbrack`$), bleeding foreign exchange out of the current account and compounding Paris Agreement NDC liabilities to deliver low useful mechanical or thermal work [Ref 9, 40, 46].
* **Domestic Renewable Electricity ($`N`$):** Electric drive motors, EVs, and industrial/residential heat pumps achieve $`e_N \ge 0.85`$ (or thermal coefficients of performance exceeding unity) [Ref 9, 46]. Electrification is therefore not an abatement drag on total factor productivity; it is a **three-fold step-change in end-use exergy efficiency**—provided domestic renewable generation and dry-year firming capacity ($`K_N`$) expand ahead of machine demand and are not throttled by wholesale gentailer oligopoly markups ($`M_{\mathrm{olig}}`$) [Ref 39, 49].

### 2.2 Spatial Dual-Fuel Heterogeneity and External Balance (Labrousse & Perdereau)

Labrousse and Perdereau (2026 [Ref 23]) demonstrate in a dynamic spatial heterogeneous-agent framework for France—whose ~93% low-carbon nuclear/hydro electricity baseload paired with imported rural and industrial fossil dependence closely mirrors New Zealand's hydro-plus-imported-oil structure [Ref 39]—that **geography dominates income** in determining fossil-fuel exposure. Total energy behaves as a household necessity, while fossil-fuel intensity varies sharply across urban-to-rural space due to commuting distances, building stock, and regional industrial composition [Ref 23, 50].

Embedding Labrousse and Perdereau's dual-fuel structure—sharpened by Keen and Ayres's exergy conversion weights $`(e_N, e_F)`$ [Ref 9]—directly into `IGWelfare`'s 4-region spine (`Rural`, `Small`, `Large`, `Auckland`) accomplishes two theoretical objectives simultaneously:

1. **Spatial Non-Homotheticity & Dual-Fuel Exergy Bundles:** Each region *k* carries an incompressible subsistence exergy requirement $`\bar{e}(k)`$ in household utility alongside region-specific CES weights $`\gamma_h(k)`$ and $`\gamma_y(k)`$ governing the split between domestic electricity ($`N`$) and imported fossil fuel ($`F`$, subject to ETS/carbon pricing $`\tau_F`$) [Ref 23, 39, 50]. On the regional production side, useful exergy $`X_{s,k}`$ in sector *s* aggregates electric and fossil throughput as:

$$
X_{s,k} = \left\lbrack \left(1 - \gamma_{s,k}\right)\left(e_N N_{s,k}\right)^{\frac{\epsilon_y - 1}{\epsilon_y}} + \gamma_{s,k}\left(e_F F_{s,k}\right)^{\frac{\epsilon_y - 1}{\epsilon_y}} \right\rbrack^{\frac{\epsilon_y}{\epsilon_y - 1}}
$$

2. **Macro External Closure (Fossil + Defence Imports):** Aggregate imported fossil fuel demand $`F_t = \sum_{k=1}^{4} \left(F_{h,k,t} + \sum_{s} F_{s,k,t}\right) + F_{N,t} + F_{D,t}`$—where $`F_{N,t}`$ captures marginal Huntly coal and gas thermal peaking during dry hydro years [Ref 39] and $`F_{D,t}`$ captures military aviation/marine fuel—must be purchased from the Rest of the World at world price $`p_F^{\ast}`$ (plus shadow NDC carbon liability when domestic abatement falls short [Ref 40]). Financing $`p_F^{\ast} F_t`$ alongside imported defense procurement $`M_{D,t}^{\ast}`$ [Ref 59] and Net Foreign Asset ($`\mathrm{NFA}_t`$) service requires tradable export earnings [Ref 10, 23].

### 2.3 Structural Transformation in a 5-Sector Small Open Economy

To capture how generational human-capital, infrastructure, and enterprise investments resolve both domestic labor-supply bottlenecks and external balance-of-payments constraints without the dimensionality of a 19-sector ANZSIC input-output table, outer-loop production partitions into **five explicit structural sectors** (adapting Australia's *OLGA* small-open-economy logic [Ref 57] to New Zealand's institutional structure):

1. **Sector 1 — Land-Bound Primary Tradables:** Dairy, meat, horticulture, and forestry exports subject to fixed agricultural land, diminishing marginal returns, farm-gate value-chain upgrading [Ref 49, 56], and agricultural/NDC emissions pricing wedges [Ref 40].
2. **Sector 2 — Weightless / Knowledge Tradables:** High-skill intensive ($`z_h`$), unbound by land, exhibiting substantially higher value-added per joule of exergy and endogenous Arrow (1962) learning-by-doing spillovers [Ref 2, 7, 10].
3. **Sector 3 — Private Market Non-Tradables & Grid Generation:** Private residential housing, civil construction contractors, commercial freight, retail distribution, for-profit care/corporate providers, and domestic electricity generation ($`K_N`$), where private oligopoly markups reside [Ref 39, 49] and whose relative price $`p_t^N`$ pins down the **Real Exchange Rate (RER)**.
4. **Sector 4 — Public, NGO & Polycentric Civic Services (`Sector 4A` Human Co-Production + `Sector 4B` Public Network Infrastructure):** Non-market state, local-council, *Iwi/Whānau Ora*, and registered non-profit institutions delivering health, education, community social services, justice, Three Waters, and public transport/roading stewardship [Ref 29, 30, 36, 58].
5. **Sector 5 — Sovereign Defence & External Security (`Sector D`):** The New Zealand Defence Force (NZDF), Ministry of Defence capability procurement, and external security apparatus [Ref 59], isolated as its own macroeconomic lane to stress-test a **+150% defense posture expansion** (1.2% to 3.0% of GDP, +NZD 8.0B/yr; [Document 3](Document%203.md), `Trajectories 2 & 7`).

Closing the international capital account with a Schmitt-Grohé and Uribe (2003 [Ref 10]) debt-elastic sovereign risk premium,

$$
r_t = r_t^{\ast} + \psi \left(\exp\left(\frac{\mathrm{NFA}_t}{Y_t} - \bar{b}\right) - 1\right)
$$

links domestic social, fiscal, defense, and energy choices directly to international borrowing costs [Ref 1, 4, 10].

### 2.4 Anatomy of Sector 4 (Polycentric Civic Services) & Sector 5 (Sovereign Defence): Anti-Double-Counting Boundaries and New Zealand's Empirical Ledger

Modeling Sectors 4 and 5 credibly requires strict accounting hygiene so that fiscal cash transfers, private construction contracts, and Crown-funded NGO grants are **never double-counted** across sectors (`CBAx` Counting Rule #1 [Ref 33]). The boundary between Sector 3 (`Private Market Non-Tradables`) and Sector 4 (`Public, NGO & Polycentric Civic Services`) is governed by **who owns the asset, sets the price, and governs access**—not who pours the concrete:

* **Rule 1 — Pure Cash Transfers (`0%` in Sectors 4 & 5):** New Zealand Superannuation (~NZD 26.5B/yr), working-age income-support transfers (~NZD 27.0B/yr), and Crown debt interest (~NZD 10.2B/yr) are pure redistributive or financial transfers (~NZD 63.7B/yr total) already tracked in household disposable income `y_disp` and sovereign debt service ([Document 2A](Document%202A.md), [Document 2B](Document%202B.md)). Only the administrative staff at MSD and IRD who process them enter Sector 4.
* **Rule 2 — Ownership vs. Private Civil Contracting in Water & Roading:** Public network assets—all 67 territorial/unitary councils' Three Waters utilities (`Watercare`, `Wellington Water`, *Local Water Done Well* CCOs) and the state/local road, rail, and public-transport network (`NZTA Waka Kotahi`, council roading, `KiwiRail`, `AT`, `Metlink`)—sit **100% in Sector 4B** for asset ownership ($`K_{G,k}`$), network engineering, and civic tariff-setting [Ref 36]. When a water utility or `NZTA` hires private civil contractors (`Fulton Hogan`, `Downer`, `HEB`) to lay pipes or reseal highways, Sector 4B purchases gross fixed capital formation ($`I_{G,k}`$) from **Sector 3 (`Private Construction`)**, while commercial freight operators (`Mainfreight`) remain in Sector 3.
* **Rule 3 — Netting Crown-to-NGO Contracts & Separating Corporate Chains in Health & Education:** Public hospitals (`Te Whatu Ora`), specialist mental health/CAMHS, *Iwi/Whānau Ora* providers, community/charity health trusts (`St John`, `Plunket`, `Barnardos`), state and state-integrated schools, *Kura Kaupapa Māori*, public tertiary institutions, and community ECE (*Kindergartens, Playcentre, Kōhanga Reo, A'oga Amata*) sit in **Sector 4A** (~85%–88% of Health and ~92%–95% of Education) [Ref 30, 37]. For-profit corporate ECE chains, commercial private surgical hospitals, and listed retirement-village operators (`Ryman`, `Summerset`) remain in **Sector 3** because they maximize private equity returns $`R_k`$ [Ref 2]. Crucially, the ~40% of non-profit sector revenue derived from Crown service contracts is **netted out upon consolidation** so Crown Vote Health/Education dollars and NGO delivery are counted exactly once [Ref 30].

### 2.5 Four Structural Insights Emerging from the Sector 4 & Sector 5 Ledger

Putting real 2025/2026 New Zealand revenue, headcount, and capital numbers onto Sectors 4 and 5 reveals four macroeconomic mechanisms that directly govern `IGWelfare`'s 60-year transition dynamics:

1. **The Headcount-vs.-Capex Dichotomy Inside Sector 4 (`4A` Human Care vs. `4B` Pipes & Roads):**
   Health, Education, and Grassroots NGOs (`Rows 4A-1 to 4A-3`) employ **~80%+ of Sector 4's human workforce (~540,000+ paid workers)**, making them classic **Baumol (1967 [Ref 58]) labor-intensive sectors** where metropolitan housing affordability (`Q_k`) for nurses and *Kaiako* ([Document 2T](Document%202T.md)) and **Ostrom (1996 [Ref 29]) community co-production** govern unit delivery costs:

$$
Y_{G,k}^{\mathrm{dev}} = A_{\mathrm{poly}}(S_t) \left\lbrack \omega_G \left(H_{G,k}^{\mathrm{dev}}\right)^{\rho_g} + \left(1 - \omega_G\right) \left(L_{u,\mathrm{civic},k} + K_{\mathrm{wh\bar{a}nau},k}\right)^{\rho_g} \right\rbrack^{1/\rho_g}
$$

   Conversely, Roading and Three Waters (`Rows 4B-1 & 4B-2`) employ relatively few direct public staff (~25,000 in network engineering/operations) yet steward **hundreds of billions of dollars in physical public capital ($`K_{G,k}`$)**—purchasing civil construction from Sector 3 and suffering severe nonlinear failure penalties when short-term budgets raid preventative maintenance (as proven in the September 2026 *Moa Point Review* [Ref 36]).
2. **The Ageing Volunteer Base Proof (Why Universal NZ Super at Age 64/65 Anchors Sector 4A):**
   The September 2026 *JBWere / University of Waikato For-Purpose Sector Report* [Ref 30] establishes that New Zealand's non-profit sector contributes **NZD 25.8B/yr (5.7% of GDP—larger than agriculture, forestry, and fishing at 5.1%)** once **165 million annual volunteer hours (79,000 FTEs)** are counted, *yet volunteer headcount fell 24%, concentrating the nation's volunteer workload onto a shrinking, ageing cohort of older citizens*. In `IGWelfare`, this provides direct macro-empirical validation for Trajectory 3 vs. Trajectory 8 ([Document 3](Document%203.md)): raising the NZ Superannuation eligibility age to 70 does not merely strip grandmother childcare (`K_whanau`) from young families—it liquidates the unpaid volunteer co-production base $`L_{u,\mathrm{civic},k}`$ of Sector 4A, forcing the Crown to replace lost community capacity with $`3\times\mathrm{–}5\times`$ more expensive state remediation [Ref 26, 29, 30, 58].
3. **Internal Labor Release Within Sector 4A (`Reactive` -> `Capacitating` Without Labor Crowding-Out):**
   Sector 4A produces both **Capacitating Investment ($`Y_{G,k}^{\mathrm{dev}}`$)** and **Reactive Remediation ($`Y_{G,k}^{\mathrm{rem}}`$, Gate 6 Exits 1, 2, and 4)** out of the same domestic pool of nurses, social workers, psychologists, and public servants. Compressing the struggling youth launch share from **22.0% to 1.5%** in `SS1 -> SS2` cuts demand for reactive hospital nights, state care, and corrections by over 75% [Ref 16, 37]—**internally releasing tens of thousands of skilled Sector 4A workers** into preventative early learning, primary health, and private Sector 2 enterprises without overheating domestic wages.
4. **Why a +150% Defence Expansion (`Sector 5`: 1.2% -> 3.0% of GDP) Demands Both Exergy Transition and Human-Capital Scaling:**
   Isolating Sector 5 [Ref 59] reveals why an unfunded +NZD 8.0B/yr defense uplift sinks `SS0` household welfare to **-21.69% CE**, whereas the unified 4-coalition compact sustains the exact same 3.0%-of-GDP posture at **+21.18% CE** ([Document 3](Document%203.md), `Table A.3`):
   * *External FX Squeeze:* Because major defense platforms and sustainment carry a foreign import share of $`\theta_{M,\mathrm{def}} \in \lbrack 0.55, 0.70 \rbrack`$ (~NZD 4.5B–5.6B/yr in direct new FX outflows) plus military fuel $`F_{D,t}`$, adding Sector 5 expansion onto an oil-importing status quo widens the current account deficit and steepens sovereign borrowing spreads $`r_t`$. Slashing ~NZD 19.5B/yr of civilian fossil fuel imports via Keen–Labrousse electrification [Ref 9, 23, 46] frees the exact balance-of-payments headroom required to import defense capabilities without currency or spread crises.
   * *Domestic Technical-Talent Squeeze:* Doubling defense readiness pulls technical trades and engineers ($`H_{D,k,t}`$) directly out of the `h_4`–`h_7` pool. Without expanding the thriving youth pipeline (`23.5% -> 65.0%`) and stemming trans-Tasman youth emigration (`10.93% -> 7.60%`), Sector 5 poaches scarce engineers directly from Sector 2 exporters and Sector 4B water/grid upgrades.

### 2.6 Paid vs. Unpaid Care and the Time-Energy Squeeze (Marilyn Waring)

Completing the micro-foundation on the household side, we formalize Marilyn Waring (1988 [Ref 47]) via a Becker–Gronau household time trichotomy [Ref 28], splitting the normalized period time endowment ($`T = 1`$) into **market labor ($`\ell_m`$)**, **unpaid household/*whānau* and community volunteer production ($`\ell_u = \ell_{u,\mathrm{home}} + \ell_{u,\mathrm{civic}}`$)**, and **pure leisure ($`1 - \ell_m - \ell_u`$)** [Ref 28, 30, 50]:

* Market non-energy goods and services ($`c_m`$, notably Sector 3 private non-tradables priced at $`p_t^N`$) combine with unpaid home production ($`c_u = A_u \ell_{u,\mathrm{home}}^{\theta_u}`$) via a high-substitutability CES aggregator ($`\sigma_c > 1`$), while unpaid family and community hours feed directly into child skill transitions (`HumanCapital_Lifecycle_aprimeFn.m`), regional $`K_{\mathrm{wh\bar{a}nau}}`$, and Sector 4A polycentric co-production $`L_{u,\mathrm{civic},k}`$ [Ref 11, 29, 30, 47].
* When `Rural` and low-income urban families face high incompressible fossil/electricity bills $`\bar{e}(k)`$ and elevated rents $`r_{h,k}`$, adults must work extra survival market hours $`\ell_m`$, crowding out $`\ell_u`$ and triggering Stage-2 dynamic-complementarity losses ($`\varphi_2 = -0.95`$) ([Document 2T](Document%202T.md) [Ref 11, 23, 46]). Household electrification [Ref 46] and Sector 4A universal ECE relieve budget poverty and time poverty simultaneously.

## 4. Statement of Unique Contribution to the Economic Literature

Existing quantitative macroeconomic policy models partition human capability, public/civic governance, biophysical energy, and small-open-economy dynamics into four disconnected literatures:

* **Multi-sector OLG trade and fiscal models** (such as Australia's *OLGA* or *OG-Core* [Ref 57]) capture small-open-economy capital flows, sovereign debt, and industry taxation, but treat early human capital as exogenous or adult-only, model government spending as an unproductive scalar sink ("throwing tax dollars into the ocean") or uniform public capital, omit unpaid care, and represent energy via high-elasticity neoclassical cost shares that miss physical capacity bottlenecks [Ref 9].
* **Spatial heterogeneous-agent climate and resource models** (Hassler, Krusell, & Olovsson, 2021 [Ref 10]; Labrousse & Perdereau, 2026 [Ref 23]) capture geographic fossil-fuel lock-in and short-run putty-clay energy complementarities, but operate in infinitely lived Bewley–Huggett–Aiyagari environments without lifecycle skill formation, polycentric public/NGO service production, or endogenous structural transformation across tradable export sectors.
* **Lifecycle skill-formation and home-production OLG models** (Cunha, Heckman, & Schennach, 2010 [Ref 11]; Lee & Seshadri, 2019 [Ref 12]; Gronau, 1977 [Ref 28]; Waring, 1988 [Ref 47]) formalize early childhood dynamic complementarities and non-market caregiving time, but treat the macroeconomy as a closed, single-good aggregate detached from balance-of-payments constraints, real exchange rates, Baumol public-sector labor clearing, and physical exergy conversion.
* **Institutional & public-administration theories of polycentric co-production and output measurement** (Ostrom, 1996 [Ref 29]; Atkinson, 2005; Baumol, 1967 [Ref 58]) demonstrate empirically why community/NGO co-production outperforms monolithic state remediation and why SNA input-cost accounting mismeasures defensive expenditures, yet have rarely been embedded inside a dynamic stochastic general-equilibrium OLG solver.

By integrating these four traditions within a computationally tractable 4-region + `Abroad`, 60-year transition framework, `IGWelfare` establishes two novel results in computational public economics:

1. **Biophysical Exergy and Polycentric Human-Capital Formation as Joint Small-Open-Economy Complements:** The model formally connects micro-level household time and subsistence-energy constraints—where distributed electrification [Ref 9, 46] and polycentric community/state care infrastructure [Ref 11, 29, 30] jointly relieve the rural and low-income squeeze on unpaid *whānau* caregiving and volunteering—to macro-level external solvency. Shifting from low-exergy imported fossil fuels to high-exergy domestic renewables, alongside cohort skill-upgrading out of land-bound primary commodities into weightless tradables, endogenously stabilizes the current account, real exchange rate, and sovereign borrowing spread—even while absorbing a +150% external defense expansion [Ref 10, 23, 59].
2. **Endogenous Resolution of Baumol's Fiscal Cost Disease via Reactive-to-Capacitating Labor Release:** By modeling public, NGO, and infrastructure services explicitly alongside unpaid volunteer co-production (`79,000 FTEs` [Ref 30]) and distinguishing capacitating human investment from reactive remediation (`Exits 1, 2, and 4`), the model demonstrates quantitatively how early-lifecycle social investment cures Baumol's cost disease from the demand side—releasing skilled labor from defensive state remediation into high-value care and tradable enterprise without double-counting fiscal transfers or expanding the GPU household state space [Ref 29, 47, 58].

## Epistemic & Deliberative Architecture for the Five Citizens' Assemblies: What Is Knowable, Decidable, Defensible, and Achievable

Expanding `IGWelfare` to a 5-sector, biophysical-exergy, and care-inclusive Small Open Economy (`Compute_5Sector_Exergy_Equilibrium.m`, `Compute_ThreeTier_NationalAccounts_Ledger.m`, `Compute_Public_Asset_Stewardship_Ledger.m`, and `Compute_Contract_Shock_Sensitivity_Ledger.m`) serves a constitutional purpose beyond academic completeness. As set out in [Document 1F](./Document%201F.md), breaking Aotearoa New Zealand's partisan "Stop–Go" deadlock requires partitioning national renewal across **Five Parallel Citizens' Assemblies**—each deliberating on a tractable domain while locking its recommendations together through an open-source **Economic Clearinghouse**.

For everyday citizens selected by civic sortition to deliberate with confidence—and without being overwhelmed by technocratic jargon or point forecasts that inevitably shift between model releases and assembly convening—an honest decision-support platform must draw four sharp epistemic boundaries: **what is knowable**, **what is decidable**, **what is defensible**, and **what is achievable**.

### 1. What Is Knowable: Biophysical Laws, Stock-Flow Identities, and Convex Decay

Citizens should never be asked to "vote" on laws of thermodynamics or balance-sheet identities, nor should political rhetoric be allowed to obscure them. Four structural relationships in our Phase II architecture represent **knowable system physics** whose sign and curvature hold across wide parameter tolerances:

1. **Thermodynamic Exergy Asymmetry ($`e_N \gg e_F`$):** Under the Second Law of Thermodynamics, domestic renewable electricity applied to heat pumps, electric drivetrains, and industrial motors converts primary energy into useful physical work at roughly three times the efficiency of combusting imported fossil hydrocarbons ($`e_N / e_F \approx 2.9`$). Replacing imported oil, diesel, and coal with firmed domestic electricity permanently compresses foreign-exchange leakage while expanding productive output—a physical wedge no tax slogan can replicate.
2. **The "Tooth Cavity" Convexity of Deferred Maintenance:** Across physical water and roading networks (`Sector 4B`), early human development (`Sector 4A-2`), preventative primary health (`Sector 4A-1`), and civic trust (`Sector 4A-3`), deferring routine upkeep does not shift costs linearly across time at the bond yield. Decay compounds geometrically: pennies of prevention deferred become dollars of remediation and tens of dollars of acute emergency intervention. Any accounting rule (such as 3-year cash OBEGAL) that books deferred maintenance as a "saving" while omitting the accruing repair liability systematically misleads decision-makers.
3. **The Accounting Mirage of Distressed Network Privatization:** Selling natural-monopoly public infrastructure (`Sector 4B` water, grid, or transport trunks) at a fire-sale discount to flatter short-term Crown cash balances is an accounting illusion whenever the underlying failure is governance rather than asset obsolescence. Because private monopoly buyers price regulated or oligopolistic markups ($`M_{\mathrm{olig}} > 1`$) onto downstream households and tradable exporters while extinguishing the public's option value via steep buyback premia, long-horizon societal present value turns sharply negative even as Year 1–3 Crown cash appears positive.
4. **Why Conventional SNA GDP Misprices Care and "Anti-Bads":** Conventional `Tier 1` System of National Accounts (`Y_SNA`) counts emergency hospitalisations, youth justice beds, and burst-pipe excavations as positive economic output while pricing unpaid household care and grassroots volunteer co-production at zero. Only by tracking `Tier 1` (`Y_SNA`), `Tier 2` (the **Atkinson–Waring Satellite Account** `Y_AW`, pricing non-market care and volunteer time at replacement cost while deducting defensive remedial regret `Y_rem`), and `Tier 3` (intergenerational welfare `CE%`) side-by-side can citizens distinguish genuine wealth creation from expensive social firefighting.

### 2. What Is Decidable: The Normative Trade-Off Space Belonging Strictly to Citizens

Once knowable physics and honest accounting clear away false economies, models must stop dictating and hand the pen to democratic deliberation. Within the feasible frontier, four core value choices are **genuinely decidable** only by representative citizens weighing competing goods:

A steeper ramp cools urban rents faster for young renters; a 10-year smooth ramp gives leveraged family homeowners a predictable decade to adjust without balance-sheet distress.
3. **Institutional Models for Public-Asset Turnaround (`Assembly 5` & `Assembly 3`):** When public networks or social agencies underperform, which blend of independent commercial-engineering boards, Ostrom-style user/community co-governance, *Te Tiriti* partnership, and demand-side vouchers (such as the Joiner Passport) best restores accountability without triggering either bureaucratic capture or private monopoly extraction?

Finally, grand reform blueprints routinely fail the test of **achievability** because they assume the state can hire tens of thousands of non-existent teachers, nurses, and engineers on Day 1 or borrow limitlessly against thin international capital markets.
